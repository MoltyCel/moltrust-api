## Article 6 — Classification rules for high-risk AI systems
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Art 6`.

- **6(1)** — Annex I product-safety route. High-risk where **both**: (a) the AI system is a safety component of, or is itself, a product covered by the Union harmonisation legislation in **Annex I**; and (b) that product must undergo **third-party conformity assessment** under that Annex I legislation.
- **6(2)** — "AI systems referred to in **Annex III** shall be considered to be high-risk."
- **6(3)** — Derogation: an Annex III system is **not** high-risk where it "does not pose a significant risk of harm to the health, safety or fundamental rights of natural persons, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision making", if any of: **(a)** narrow procedural task; **(b)** improves the result of a previously completed human activity; **(c)** detects decision-making patterns/deviations without replacing or influencing the human assessment without proper review; **(d)** preparatory task to an Annex III assessment. **Override:** "an AI system referred to in Annex III shall **always** be considered to be high-risk where the AI system performs **profiling of natural persons**."
- **6(4)** — Provider claiming the 6(3) exemption **must document** the assessment before placing on market and is subject to registration under Art 49(2).
- **6(5)** — Commission guidelines on practical implementation due **no later than 2 February 2026**.

## Article 7 — Amendments to Annex III (usable as risk-scoring criteria)
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Art 7`. The **Art 7(2)** criteria are the feature set for gap/risk scoring:
(a) intended purpose; (b) extent of use; (c) nature/amount of data incl. special-category personal data;
(d) autonomy and possibility of human override; (e) harm already caused; (f) potential extent/intensity of harm and number of persons affected; (g) dependence of affected persons on the outcome; (h) power imbalance / vulnerability; (i) corrigibility/reversibility of the outcome (adverse impacts on health/safety/fundamental rights are **not** deemed easily reversible); (j) magnitude/likelihood of benefit; (k) whether existing Union law already provides redress/mitigation.

## Annex III — High-risk use-cases (Art 6(2)) — verbatim list
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Annex III`. Chapeau: "High-risk AI systems pursuant to Article 6(2) are the AI systems listed in any of the following areas:"

1. **Biometrics** (where permitted): (a) remote biometric identification (excludes pure biometric verification/one-to-one confirmation); (b) biometric categorisation by sensitive/protected attributes; (c) emotion recognition.
2. **Critical infrastructure**: AI as safety components in management/operation of critical digital infrastructure, road traffic, or supply of water, gas, heating, electricity.
3. **Education & vocational training**: (a) access/admission/assignment; (b) evaluating learning outcomes / steering learning; (c) assessing appropriate level of education; (d) monitoring/detecting prohibited behaviour during tests.
4. **Employment & workers' management**: (a) recruitment/selection (targeted ads, filtering applications, evaluating candidates); (b) decisions on terms, promotion/termination, task allocation by behaviour/traits, performance/behaviour monitoring.
5. **Essential private & public services**: (a) public-benefit eligibility (incl. healthcare) — grant/reduce/revoke/reclaim; (b) creditworthiness / credit scoring (except fraud detection); (c) risk assessment & pricing for life and health insurance; (d) emergency-call triage / first-response dispatch priority.
6. **Law enforcement** (where permitted): (a) victim-risk assessment; (b) polygraphs/similar; (c) evidence-reliability assessment; (d) offending/re-offending risk (beyond mere profiling); (e) profiling per Art 3(4) Dir (EU) 2016/680.
7. **Migration, asylum & border control** (where permitted): (a) polygraphs/similar; (b) risk assessment of entrants; (c) examination of asylum/visa/residence applications & evidence reliability; (d) detection/recognition/identification of persons (except travel-document verification).
8. **Administration of justice & democratic processes**: (a) judicial fact/law research & application (and similar ADR); (b) influencing elections/referenda/voting behaviour (excludes purely administrative/logistical campaign tools not exposing natural persons to output).

## Article 5 — Prohibited practices (unacceptable-risk tier)
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Art 5(1)`. Chapeau: "The following AI practices shall be prohibited:"
- (a) subliminal/manipulative/deceptive techniques materially distorting behaviour causing significant harm;
- (b) exploiting vulnerabilities (age, disability, social/economic situation) causing significant harm;
- (c) **social scoring** leading to detrimental/unfavourable treatment;
- (d) individual **predictive policing** based solely on profiling/personality;
- (e) **untargeted scraping** of facial images to build/expand facial-recognition databases;
- (f) **emotion inference** in workplace and education (except medical/safety);
- (g) **biometric categorisation** to deduce race, political opinions, trade-union membership, religious/philosophical beliefs, sex life, sexual orientation;
- (h) **'real-time' remote biometric identification** in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement (except narrow (i)–(iii) exceptions with safeguards Art 5(2)–(7)).

## Article 50 — Transparency obligations (limited-risk tier)
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Art 50`. Duties independent of high-risk status; stack on top of Chapter III (50(6)):
- 50(1) chatbot/AI-interaction disclosure; 50(2) synthetic audio/image/video/text machine-readable marking; 50(3) emotion-recognition / biometric-categorisation deployer disclosure; 50(4) deep-fake disclosure; 50(5) at latest at first interaction/exposure.

## Article 95 — Minimal-risk residual tier
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Art 95(1)`. No formal "minimal risk" definition exists; it is the residual category (not prohibited, not high-risk, not caught by Art 50). Chapter III requirements are **voluntary** via codes of conduct.

## Classification logic (inputs → tier), first-match-wins
1. Matches any Art 5(1)(a)–(h) → **PROHIBITED** (stop).
2. Else Annex I product + third-party conformity assessment (Art 6(1)) → **HIGH-RISK**.
3. Else use-case in an Annex III area (Art 6(2)) → presumptively **HIGH-RISK**, unless Art 6(3) derogation (a)–(d) applies — **never** derogated if it performs **profiling of natural persons**. Provider claiming derogation must document + register (Art 6(4)). Score with Art 7(2) criteria.
4. Else chatbot / synthetic-content / emotion-recognition / biometric-categorisation → **LIMITED** (Art 50 transparency duties).
5. Else → **MINIMAL** (Art 95, voluntary).

## Annex V — EU declaration of conformity — mandatory fields (→ VC schema)
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Annex V`. "The EU declaration of conformity referred to in Article 47, shall contain all of the following information:" (points 1–8, complete):
1. AI system name and type and any additional unambiguous reference allowing identification and traceability.
2. Name and address of the provider or, where applicable, of their authorised representative.
3. Statement that the declaration is issued under the sole responsibility of the provider.
4. Statement that the AI system is in conformity with this Regulation and any other relevant Union law providing for the declaration.
5. Where personal data is processed, a statement of compliance with Reg (EU) 2016/679, Reg (EU) 2018/1725 and Dir (EU) 2016/680.
6. References to any relevant harmonised standards or other common specification in relation to which conformity is declared.
7. Where applicable, name & identification number of the notified body, description of the conformity-assessment procedure, and identification of the certificate.
8. Place and date of issue, name and function of the signatory, indication of for/on behalf of whom signed, a signature.

→ These 8 map 1:1 to the `credentialSubject` fields of the `MolTrustConformityDeclaration` VC.

## Article 73 — Reporting of serious incidents (→ deadline tracker)
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Art 73`.
- **73(1)** — Providers of high-risk AI systems placed on the Union market **shall report any serious incident** to the market surveillance authorities of the Member State(s) where it occurred.
- **73(2) — GENERAL RULE:** report "immediately after the provider has established a causal link … or the reasonable likelihood of such a link, and, in any event, **not later than 15 days** after the provider or, where applicable, the deployer, becomes aware of the serious incident." Period takes account of severity.
- **73(3) — CRITICAL-INFRA / WIDESPREAD:** for a widespread infringement or a serious incident as defined in **Art 3(49)(b)** — "immediately, and **not later than two days**" after becoming aware.
- **73(4) — DEATH OF A PERSON:** report "immediately … but **not later than 10 days**" after becoming aware.
- **73(5)** — an incomplete initial report followed by a complete report is allowed.
- **73(6)** — provider must investigate without delay (risk assessment + corrective action); must not alter the system in a way affecting evaluation before informing authorities.
- **73(8)** — market surveillance authority acts within **seven days** of receiving the notification (authority-side, not the provider deadline).

**Verdict on the PBA "15 days":** CONFIRMED as the **general** deadline (73(2)) — but it is **not the only** deadline. Implement three tiers: **15d** (general), **2d** (Art 3(49)(b) critical-infra / widespread), **10d** (death). Deadline = `awareness_date + {2|10|15} days` selected by severity/type.

## Article 3(49) — "serious incident" definition
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Art 3(49)`. An incident/malfunction that directly or indirectly leads to:
(a) the death of a person, or serious harm to a person's health; (b) a serious and irreversible disruption of the management or operation of critical infrastructure; (c) infringement of Union-law obligations protecting fundamental rights; (d) serious harm to property or the environment.
(73(3)→(49)(b); 73(4)→death under (49)(a); 73(7)/(9)/(10)→(49)(c).)

## Article 12 — Record-keeping *(EC-Service-Desk-verified — see provenance caveat)*
Pin: `Reg (EU) 2024/1689, Art 12`.
- **12(1)** — "High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system."
- **12(2)** — logging shall enable recording of events relevant for (a) identifying Art 79(1) risk situations / substantial modification; (b) post-market monitoring (Art 72); (c) monitoring operation per Art 26(5).
- **12(3)** — for Annex III point 1(a) (remote biometric ID) systems, at minimum: (a) period of each use; (b) reference database checked against; (c) matched input data; (d) identity of the natural persons verifying results (Art 14(5)).

→ Product wording stays exactly: **"supports Article 12 logging"** (Console-Auftrag §1). Art 12(3) minimum-set is specific to remote-biometric-ID systems, not all high-risk systems.
